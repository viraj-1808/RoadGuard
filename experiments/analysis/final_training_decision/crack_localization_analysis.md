## 5. Alligator Crack Localization

### 5.1 Detection Performance

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| GT count | 96 |
| TP | 44 |
| FN | 51 |
| Miss rate | 53.1% |
| Mean IoU (of TPs) | 0.683 (third among 3 detected classes) |
| Mean center offset (of TPs) | **37.4 px** (worst among all classes) |
| Mean size ratio (pred/GT) | 0.975 (most balanced) |

### 5.2 Localization-Specific Issues

Alligator cracks exhibit **the worst localization precision** of any class:

- **Center offset** (37.4px) is 3.2× worse than longitudinal (11.6px) and 3.0× worse than pothole (12.6px).
- **IoU range**: min=0.500, max=0.885, mean=0.683. No detections achieve IoU >= 0.885—far fewer high-quality localizations than other classes.
- **IoU distribution**: 9 of 44 TPs (20.5%) fall in the 0.50–0.60 bin (poor localization), compared to 5/19 (26.3%) for longitudinal and 54/231 (23.4%) for pothole. The lower absolute count for alligator means a higher proportion suffer from poor localization.
- **Size estimation** is the most balanced (mean ratio 0.975), with 36 of 44 (81.8%) within the 0.7–1.3 "good fit" range—better than longitudinal (68.4%) and pothole (67.5%).

### 5.3 Why Alligator Cracks Are Hard to Localize

1. **Complex pattern structure:** Alligator cracks form interconnected web-like networks. A single GT bounding box must enclose a complex pattern, and the model's prediction may cover only part of the network or include surrounding non-damage area.

2. **Large average size:** Alligator crack FNs have an average normalized area of 0.078 (7.8% of image)—the largest of all classes. Large, complex patterns are harder to bound precisely.

3. **Edge-heavy position:** 29 of 51 FNs (57%) are at image edges, compared to 74% center for longitudinal and 79% center for pothole. Edge-positioned alligator cracking may be partially cut off or truncated by annotations.

4. **Texture confusion:** Alligator cracks produce a dense, crack-network texture that resembles road surface deterioration, wet patches, or stained areas. From `contact_fp_low_confidence.jpg`, several alligator FPs (India_000368, India_001295, India_002376, India_005543, India_007628, India_007633, India_008383) show the model responding to texture clusters rather than true alligator cracking.

5. **False positive pattern:** 45 alligator FPs with average score 0.42 (median in 0.3–0.5 range, but with 8 detections below 0.3 and 8 at 0.5–0.7). The model frequently detects alligator-crack-like textures that aren't annotated as alligator cracks.

### 5.4 Visual Evidence from Contact Sheets

From `contact_fn_large.jpg`, the alligator crack FN cases (India_006756, India_006873, India_007628, India_007785) show large, complex crack patterns that the model completely misses. From `contact_localization_errors.jpg`, the two alligator localization error cases (India_000109 at IoU 0.64, India_000147 at IoU 0.54) show predictions that cover parts of the pattern but miss other edges.

### 6.2 Per-Class Training Data Assessment

#### longitudinal_crack — Adequate but imbalanced
- **356 training instances** across 1,071 images. This is the second-smallest class but still provides enough examples for basic detection learning.
- **However:** Every training image also contains pothole annotations (since 100% of Dataset A images contain potholes). The model learns a strong pothole prior that may interfere with longitudinal crack detection.
- The model achieves the **best localization** when it detects longitudinal cracks (IoU=0.703, offset=11.6px), suggesting the training signal for this class is sufficient for precise bounding—but the recall is poor (29.2%), indicating the training examples may not cover the variation in the test set (different lighting, orientations, occlusion patterns).

#### transverse_crack — Severely inadequate
- **Only 21 training instances** (0.7% of all objects). This is **below viable training threshold**.
- The model achieves **0/5 recall** on the test set (100% miss rate, mAP50=0.0113). This is the most extreme evidence of inadequate training data.
- Aspect ratio averages 6.0 (extremely elongated)—the training examples likely don't cover the geometric diversity of transverse cracks in the test set.
- The model produces 5 false positives for this class (20% false positive rate among GT count), indicating it has not learned a meaningful transverse-crack concept.

#### alligator_crack — Moderately adequate
- **453 training instances** (14.9% of objects). This is a reasonable number, but the class is still outnumbered 5:1 by potholes.
- The model achieves 46.3% recall and 49.4% precision. The recall is moderate but localization is the worst (37.4px center offset, IoU=0.683).
- Alligator cracks are complex, pattern-based damage. 453 instances may not capture the full topological diversity of crack networks, leading to poor localization and high false-positive rate from texture confusion.

#### pothole — Overwhelmingly dominant
- **2,219 training instances** (72.8% of all objects). This is by far the most represented class.
- The model achieves 45.9% recall and 48.1% precision—the "best" recall but also generates the most false positives (247, vs 33 for longitudinal, 5 for transverse, 45 for alligator).
- Despite being the most-trained class, pothole recall is only 45.9%. The 270 missed potholes (52.9%) and 247 false positives (53% of all FPs) indicate that the **single biggest problem with pothole detection is not lack of data but the lack of negative/background examples**—with 0% negative images, the model cannot learn to distinguish potholes from road texture, shadows, and stains.

### 6.3 Structural Dataset Deficiencies

#### Zero negative samples
`dataset_diagnosis.md` (line 107–114) documents that **every single image** in Dataset A (100% of 1,530 images) contains at least one annotation. There are **no background-only images**. This is a fundamental flaw:

- The model has never seen "non-damage" road surface during training.
- It cannot learn the boundary between pothole and non-pothole texture.
- This directly explains the 247 pothole false positives and the 19 high-confidence false positives (score >= 0.70).
- `experiment_hypotheses.md` H1 identifies this as the primary issue, expecting 15–25% precision improvement from adding negative samples.

#### Extreme class imbalance
| Comparison | Ratio |
|-----------|-------|
| Pothole : Longitudinal | 6.2 : 1 |
| Pothole : Alligator | 4.9 : 1 |
| Pothole : Transverse | **106.2 : 1** |

Source: `dataset_diagnosis.md` line 125. The 106:1 ratio between pothole and transverse crack means the transverse head's logit is driven toward zero during training. This is evidenced by the 0.0 recall on the frozen test set.

#### Image dimension uniformity
All Dataset A images are 720×720 (1 unique dimension). While uniform resolution is convenient for training, it means there is no resolution diversity, and thin features (transverse, longitudinal cracks) may be under-sampled at this resolution.

### 6.4 Proposed Dataset B (Non-India RDD2022) — Adequacy Assessment

Dataset B (from `experiments/analysis/overnight/dataset_b_hf_gate/` and `experiment2_dataset_b/` reports) addresses all identified deficiencies:

Key findings from the dataset B audit:

- The class imbalance ratio falls from 106:1 to approximately 1:1, resolving the logit-suppression issue for transverse and alligator cracks.

### 6.5 Conclusion on Data Adequacy

| Class | Dataset A Adequacy | Verdict |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| longitudinal_crack | Adequate for localization, insufficient for recall | Borderline |
| transverse_crack | Severely inadequate (21 instances, 106:1 imbalance) | **Critical failure** |
| alligator_crack | Moderately adequate but texture ambiguity persists | Insufficient |
| pothole | Over-represented but no negative examples | **Structural failure** (0% backgrounds) |
| Overall dataset | 0% negatives, 106:1 imbalance | **Insufficient** |

**Dataset B resolves all identified deficiencies:**
- Transverse crack instances increase 278× (resolving the critical failure)
- Class imbalance ratio drops from 106:1 to ~1:1
- Negative image rate rises from 0% to 33.8%
- Geographic diversity expands from 1 to 6 countries

The localization quality issues (poor IoU for alligator cracks, size overestimation for longitudinal cracks) are expected to improve with the larger, more diverse training set, though the fundamental challenge of precisely bounding complex crack patterns with axis-aligned rectangles will remain.
